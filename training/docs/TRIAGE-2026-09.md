# Triage of the September 2026 inspection photographs

Sixteen handheld inspection photographs, 3264×1836, taken 3, 4 and 7 September
2026 across Nottinghamshire. This is everything the project currently has.

**Result: none of the sixteen can be used as a whole frame, in either
direction.** Seven usable regions can be cut out of six of them — six hard
negatives and one positive. The per-image verdicts are in
`dataset/triage.csv`; the cuts are in `dataset/crops.csv`.

## Why whole frames fail

Every one of the sixteen carries survey marking paint on the road. Measured,
not eyeballed — `dlkit/paint.py` finds saturated yellow and orange, and the
road region of every frame scans between 0.02% and 0.65% paint.

That paint is sprayed *around the defects*. It is therefore perfectly
correlated with the thing the model is meant to find, and that breaks both
possible uses:

- **As positives**, the model learns that a pothole is a thing with yellow
  brackets round it. True of every training image, false of every road. It
  would then score beautifully on a held-out slice of these same photographs
  and find nothing in the field.
- **As hard negatives** it is worse. A hard negative is an image with an empty
  label file — an assertion that there is nothing to find. These frames all
  contain potholes, so the assertion would be false, and the model would learn
  that a marked pothole is background.

There is no third option for a whole frame. That is why all sixteen stay on
`hold`.

Two further problems, which cropping does not fix and which matter for how far
this data can be trusted:

- **Scale.** These are handheld at one to three metres. The defects are five
  to twenty times larger in frame than a windscreen-mounted phone ever sees.
- **Perspective.** No frame is taken from a vehicle. The product's own
  geometry — bonnet height, road filling the lower two thirds, a 2340×1080
  frame stretched into a 640 square — appears nowhere in this set.

## What can be rescued

The parts of those frames with no defect and no paint in them. Cut out, they
are real hard negatives of exactly the confusions the survey suffers from.

| crop | from | what it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| `ironwork-01` | 0857 | a gully grating and an inspection cover, with a kerb and footway blocks |
| `shade-01` | 0708 | dappled tree shade on sound surface — the hardest false positive there is |
| `wet-01` | 0940 | wet coarse A-road surface with a white lane line |
| `leaves-01` | 0706 | leaves in the channel, a patch repair, low autumn sun |
| `kerb-01` | 0645 | kerb channel with weeds, beside old patching |
| `cracking-01` | 0648 | crazing and worn lining with no material lost |
| `pothole-01` | 1101a | **the one unpainted pothole in the set** — a water-filled edge failure with a clean broken lip |

Two rules the cutting follows, both enforced by `dl.py crops`:

**Crop wide, not tight.** A feature cropped tightly out of a walking-distance
photograph arrives several times larger than a driving camera would see it,
and teaches "big manhole" when the product meets small ones. A crop of roughly
a third to a half of the frame width, downscaled into the 640 square, puts a
grating at about the apparent size it has at ten metres. Crops narrower than
25% of the frame are refused.

**Check the cut, not the intention.** Every crop is re-scanned for paint after
it is made and refused if any is found. This caught `shade-01` on the first
attempt: the box chosen by eye included marks near the left kerb. Tile-scanning
the box showed the paint confined to its top-left; moving the top edge down to
y=0.58 gives a region that scans at exactly zero.

```
./tools/dl.py crops                    # report only, writes nothing
./tools/dl.py crops --confirm          # after a human has looked at them
```

The paint scan proves there is no *marking* in a crop. It cannot prove there
is no *pothole*. That is why `--confirm` exists and why nothing is written
without it.

## What this set cannot do

**It cannot produce a gold test set.** Not one of the sixteen is fit to measure
against: a test set of paint-marked, walking-distance photographs measures
paint detection at the wrong scale. `test` stays empty, which is the honest
state.

**It cannot train a detector.** One positive example is not a small dataset,
it is a single example. Augmentation multiplies one pothole into many views of
one pothole; it does not create a second pothole.

So V2 is blocked on **capture**, not on labelling or on count. The six hard
negatives are worth having and will still be worth having later — false
positives are the survey's existing complaint and these are six real ones. But
they are the negative half of a dataset whose positive half does not yet
exist.

## What unblocks it

One drive. Phone mounted, app recording, thirty minutes on roads already known
to be bad — the ones in these photographs would do, since the defects are
still there and the paint does not appear in a frame taken from twenty metres
back at speed.

That single session yields what the whole inspection set cannot: unpainted
potholes, at driving distance, from the product's own camera position,
including the plain road between them that makes up most of a real survey.

Against that, the priority order for effort is:

1. **Drive and record.** Everything else waits on this.
2. **Label the driving frames** — positives and negatives both.
3. **Hold a whole road back** as the gold test set before any training.
4. Add these seven crops to `train` as supporting material.

Steps 1 to 3 are the experiment. Step 4 is a rounding error, and doing it
first would feel like progress while changing nothing.
