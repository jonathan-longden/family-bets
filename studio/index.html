How it works

Every take is measured before it's touched: how loud the voice is, how loud the room is, where the tone sits, how much the s's spit, which notes were sung and in what key. Those measurements pick the settings, and then a single pass does the work:

  1. Room out. The quiet moments of your own recording are the fingerprint of the room, and every frequency gets turned down by however much of it is room rather than voice.
  2. Tone. A high pass under the lowest note you sang, the mud and the boxiness pulled back, presence and air put where a voice belongs.
  3. S sounds. Only the loudest few per cent of the sibilant band gets held down, so nothing else goes dull.
  4. Level. Two compressors, one slow and one quick, so the quiet words carry without the loud ones jumping.
  5. Tuning. The voice is cut into one grain per glottal pulse and laid back down at the spacing the right note asks for. The grains aren't stretched, so it still sounds like you — and vibrato survives, because only the slow part of the error is corrected.
  6. Space and loudness. A little room, then the whole thing brought to a broadcast loudness with a look-ahead limiter on the end.

Nothing is uploaded. The audio never leaves the device, there's no account, and it all works with the network off.
