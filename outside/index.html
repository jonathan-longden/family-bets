      How it decides

      Every hour of the forecast is scored out of a hundred against the things you said you would go out for. Temperature — what it feels like, so the wind is already in it — how likely rain is and how much, the gusts rather than the average wind, and whether the sun is up.

      A run of good hours is a window. Not an hour here and there: a window has to be long enough to be worth the coat, and an hour of rain in the middle of one makes it two windows rather than one with a hole in it.

      The nudge is the whole point. It goes out when a window is about to start — not at nine every morning, and not when it is already raining. It will not go twice for the same window, it will not go in your quiet hours, and it will not go more times in a day than you allow.

      What it will not do is send you out in a thunderstorm or on freezing rain, however relaxed the setting.

      Where the weather comes from. Open-Meteo, which answers this page directly and asks for no key and no account. The only thing that ever leaves the phone is a pair of coordinates, and only to them.
