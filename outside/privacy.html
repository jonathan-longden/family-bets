Privacy

Blooming Weather — the polite name on a weather app with a mouth on it.

Last updated 29 August 2026

The short version. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising and no server of ours. Your location and your settings stay on your phone. The only thing that ever leaves it is a pair of coordinates, sent to the weather service to ask what the weather is there.

What the app collects about you

Nothing. We operate no servers, hold no database and have no way to identify you. Everything the app knows is on your own device and goes away when you delete the app.

Location

  • The app asks for location permission only when you tap Use where I am, and it explains why before the phone's own prompt appears.
  • Coordinates are rounded to four decimal places — roughly eleven metres — before they are stored or sent anywhere. The forecast is the same either way, so the extra precision would be nothing but a more exact record of where you live.
  • The rounded coordinates are saved on your device so the app knows where to look next time, and are sent to the weather service with each forecast request.
  • Refusing is fine. The app works fully without location permission — search for a town instead. Nothing is nagged, hidden or withheld.
  • No location history is kept. The app stores one place, the current one, and overwrites it when you change it.

The weather service

Forecasts come from Open-Meteo, an independent weather API. The app talks to it directly from your device — nothing passes through us.

  • Forecasts: the request contains the rounded coordinates and the range of days and hours being asked for.
  • Town search: when you search for a place, the text you typed is sent to Open-Meteo's geocoding service to be matched.
  • As with any internet request, Open-Meteo's servers receive your device's IP address. What they do with it is governed by their own terms and privacy statement, not by this policy. Open-Meteo requires no key, no account and no email address, and the app sends none.

What is stored on your device

  • The place you chose — its name and rounded coordinates.
  • Your settings: units, clock format, swearing, animation, icon badge.
  • The most recent forecast, so the app opens with no signal, plus a short summary of the previous one so it can tell you what has changed.

On the web this lives in the browser's local storage. In the iOS and Android apps it lives in the app's own private storage, which no other app can read.

Analytics, tracking and advertising

There are none. No analytics SDK, no crash reporting service, no advertising identifier, no tracking pixels, no third-party cookies, no social media buttons that phone home. Nothing about how you use the app is measured or transmitted.

Notifications

The app sends none and asks for no notification permission.

Sharing

The share button draws a picture of the weather and hands it to your phone's own share sheet. Where it goes from there is your choice and your messaging app's business — the card contains the place name, the weather and the app's name, and nothing about you or your device.

Children

The app collects no personal data from anyone, of any age. It does contain strong language when the swearing setting is on; it can be turned off in Settings, and the app then uses its clean lines throughout.

Deleting your data

  • In the app: Settings → Erase everything removes the saved place, the settings and the cached forecast immediately.
  • Everything: delete the app, or clear the site data in your browser. Because nothing is held anywhere else, that is the end of it — there is no account to close and no request to make of us.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it and the new version appears here and in the app.

Contact

Questions about this policy can be raised at the project's repository, or by email at the support address given on the app's store listing.
